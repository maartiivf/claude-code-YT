# Competition map: Spanish-language faceless long-form (documentary/explainer) niches, 2025-2026

## Key Question 1: For each candidate niche, who are the main Spanish-language faceless channels, how big and how active are they (2025-2026), and what quality level?

### Takeaway
Most of the 18 niches have 1-3 large, active Spanish-language incumbents plus a long tail of small channels, many of them new and AI-made. What changed in 2025-2026 is that much of the "Spanish" supply now comes from outside Spain and Latin America. Official Spanish versions of English brands (What If, Astrum, Kurzgesagt, Veritasium, Insider, Mentour Pilot, Chubbyemu, Megaprojects, Waterline Stories, Plane Curious) compete in Spanish. So do multi-language faceless studios whose channels carry Spanish names but also have Japanese, English and other titles (e.g., "Motor de la Civilización" = "Civilization Engine" = 文明メカニズム). On top of that, YouTube's auto-dubbing and auto-translated titles let English channels (Statrys, Crayon Capital, FINAiUS, JunkBondInvestor, The Daily Navy) show up in Spanish search results.

### Cited Findings

#### Method and caveats (read first)
- **How the data was collected.** Numbers were scraped on 29-Sep-2026 from YouTube search pages and channel `/videos` pages, using an `es-ES` browser language setting.
  - Subscriber counts and views are YouTube's rounded display values (e.g., "1,3 M").
  - The "median10" and "max10" figures are the median and maximum views of the channel's 10 most recent long-form uploads.
  - The frequency figure "10 vids/Nd" means the last 10 uploads span N days.
  - "Last" is time since the most recent upload, counted from 29-Sep-2026.
  - Source for each row: the channel's `/videos` page, linked inline.
- **Faceless status is not visually verified.** I could not watch the videos, because watch pages hit a bot-check. Labels come from the channel description, the format signals in titles (animation, 3D, reconstructions, official dub) or prior knowledge, and are marked "(unverified)" where relevant.
- **How the channel's original language was checked.** Each channel page was re-fetched with a Japanese interface. Channels whose metadata stayed in Spanish are treated as Spanish-native. Channels that showed Japanese channel names and descriptions have been translated into many languages, i.e. they are multi-language network channels. Their view counts may include audio in other languages, so their views do NOT isolate Spanish demand. [Example: CivMechanica shows "文明メカニズム" in Japanese](https://www.youtube.com/@CivMechanica/videos); [Histocu stays "Historia Oculta"](https://www.youtube.com/@Histocu/videos).
- **English titles in the Japanese check do not prove a channel is English.** With an English interface, even Spanish-native channels (Homosapiens, Ecomonos, El Negocio del Fútbol) showed English titles. YouTube therefore auto-translates titles in both directions, so title language alone does not prove origin. [Homosapiens](https://www.youtube.com/@HomosapiensYT/videos), [Ecomonos](https://www.youtube.com/@ecomonos/videos)
- **Auto-dubbing now reaches all creators.** YouTube rolled out its multi-language audio feature to all creators in September 2025. Channels using it reportedly got >25% of watch time from views in languages other than the original. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/youtubes-multi-language-audio-feature-for-dubbing-videos-rolls-out-to-all-creators/); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/how-youtubes-multi-audio-feature-changes-global-growth-strategy)
- **Policy risk for AI channels.** YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content" on 15-Jul-2025. Mass-produced, template-based videos are ineligible for monetization. This matters for the many AI channels listed below. [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660)

### Inferences
- **"Faceless in Spanish" now competes with global localization.** In at least 7 niches (science, aviation, megaprojects, maritime, medical, tech, finance), official Spanish versions or multi-language network channels already occupy the space. A new Spain-based channel competes against them for Spanish search and suggested-video traffic, not only against native creators.
- **Quality level is bimodal.** At the top are well-produced incumbents (Dieck Docs, VisualPolitik, Homosapiens, Armapedia, Víctor Vic, El Negocio del Fútbol). Below them is a large tail of template-style AI channels (factory "cómo se fabrica" clones, "psicología oscura", "historia para dormir", tiny "auge y caída" channels) with medians under 5K. Channels in the middle, with consistent medians of 50K-300K, are few in most niches.
- **Spain-specific angles look under-represented.** Most mid-tier Spanish channels are LatAm-centric: Javier Ledesma (Argentina), JPaniagua (México), Universo de Fortunas (LatAm families), El Negocio del Fútbol (LatAm/Liga MX). The exceptions are Spain-native Lord Draugr, VisualPolitik and Ander Restoy. This is an inference from topics in titles, not an audience-geography measurement.

### Gaps
- Faceless status could not be verified visually; watch pages were not scraped because of bot-checks. Channels marked "likely presenter-led" (Javier Ledesma, El Mercadólogo, Gustavo Entrala, Garaje Hermético, CONSTRUCTUM) need a manual check.
- Language origin could not be checked for Vida de Lujo, Lume (@lume-channel, 310K; mixed English and Spanish titles, suggesting multi-language) and some small channels.
- It is unknown what share of views on multi-language channels comes from Spanish audio.
- Handles @VeritasiumES, @vestigioseternos and @CuadernosdeFutbol returned no data, which may be a fetch error.
- No Social Blade data was used (HTTP 403), so subscriber growth trends are not measured. Activity is inferred from upload recency only.

## Key Question 2: Where is the gap biggest (strong English demand, but few, weak or inactive Spanish channels)?

### Takeaway
The clearest gaps are:
1. **Scams / financial fraud investigations.** There is no Spanish Coffeezilla or MagnatesMedia. Small channels hit 0.7-2.6M views, and dedicated fraud channels are tiny or dead.
2. **Medical mysteries.** Chubbyemu en Español was abandoned 3 years ago with a 100K+ median. ChamoGutiérrez posts slowly, and 4-25-video channels hit 0.5-1.7M.
3. **Tech/AI company documentaries.** There is no Spanish ColdFusion; native tech-history channels have single-digit-thousand medians despite occasional 125K-594K hits.
4. **Car-brand and auto-industry business stories.** Dedicated native channels are tiny, while car-brand episodes on general channels reach 0.3-1.3M.
5. **Maritime/shipping.** No healthy native channel exists; demand is being captured by multi-language networks and English auto-dubs.

Medium gaps: business case studies (big demand but a crowded bottom), famous trials and heists, airline business, football business from a Spain angle, and billionaire/wealth documentaries.

### Inferences
- **Common pattern among native outliers:** few videos (4-35), 12-30 minute runtimes, a curiosity-gap question title ("¿Por qué…?", "El hombre que…", "Cómo X…"), and topics with global name recognition (Nike, Little Caesars, North Korea, NVIDIA, Frank Lucas). This fits a strategy of adapting English-proven topics to Spanish.
- **Several outliers upload irregularly** (Historia Oculta: 15 videos over about 3 years; Mente Pintada: 4 videos). That suggests the algorithm is rewarding individual topics, not channel authority. It lowers the barrier for a new channel, but views will be volatile.

## Key Question 4: Which niches look saturated in Spanish (to avoid)?

### Takeaway
Avoid or treat as high-competition:
- geopolitics and economics explainers;
- military technology;
- generic "cómo se fabrica" factory videos;
- "psicología oscura" / philosophy self-help;
- history "para dormir";
- generic true crime (murders);
- mainstream science & space (unless the channel is differentiated).

In each, strong incumbents with 0.5-5.5M subs post weekly or more, official dubs compete, or AI clones have pushed medians below 5K.

### Cited Findings
- **Economics / geopolitics.** VisualPolitik (3.8M, ~5 uploads/week, median10 537.5K), Memorias de Pez (2.96M, ~5/week), VisualEconomik (896K), Academia Play (3.77M), Los Ecomonos (462K, median10 382.5K), Infonimados (998K) and Memorias de Tiburón (826K) are all active in 2026. ### Inferences
- **"Saturated" does not mean "no outliers".** ABISMO and Historia Oculta broke through in space and geopolitics. But entry there needs clearly better production or a distinct format, and topic selection alone is not enough.
- **Heavy AI clone presence is a policy risk.** In "cómo se fabrica", "psicología oscura" and "para dormir", heavy AI clones plus YouTube's July-2025 "inauthentic content" rule make it a poor fit for a new AI-assisted channel. [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- **Business case studies are becoming crowded at the bottom** (dozens of 0-500-sub AI channels) while still under-supplied in the middle. Differentiation (research depth, Spain/Europe angle, better visuals) matters more than the niche choice itself.

### Gaps
- Saturation is judged from supply-side view data. There is no data on how many new channels per month enter each niche beyond channel-search snapshots.
- CPM/RPM differences between niches (e.g., finance vs sleep content) are not covered here; see the RPM notes by other researchers in the same folder.
