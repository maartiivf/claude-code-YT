# YouTube RPM/CPM by niche and by language/market, and the 2025-2026 policy environment for faceless AI-assisted long-form channels (Spanish-language focus)

Research date: 2026-09-29. All figures are USD unless stated. "RPM" = creator revenue per 1,000 total views after YouTube's 45% cut. "CPM" = advertiser cost per 1,000 monetized playbacks/impressions before the cut. Many sources below conflate the two; I flag this where it matters.

**Source-quality warning up front:** most "RPM by niche" tables on the web in 2025-2026 are SEO/tool-vendor blogs (OutlierKit, FluxNote, Lenos, Viraland, YouViral, etc.) that publish round-number ranges with no transparent methodology and often copy each other. The single most rigorous dataset found is **AIR Media-Tech's Studio-data study (300 channels, 3,595 monetized channel-months, May 2025-May 2026)**, and its numbers are **much lower** than the SEO tables. Read the SEO ranges as "US-English, top-of-range, good-month" ceilings, and AIR medians as the realistic middle.

## 1. Recent (2024-2026) reported RPM and CPM ranges by niche

### Takeaway
The only data-backed 2025-26 dataset (AIR Media-Tech, real Studio data) puts the all-niche median long-form RPM at about $2.30, with Education & Science ($10.22) and Transport/cars/aviation ($5.69) on top. The SEO tables put finance, insurance, legal and real estate at $15-55 RPM, but those figures describe top-performing US-English channels, not the median. Across every source the ranking holds: finance/insurance/legal/real estate/B2B software > business/tech/education/health > history/true crime/science/documentary > entertainment/gaming/music > kids. The spread inside one niche (up to 8x) is often larger than the gap between niches.

### Inferences
- The realistic planning number for a well-run long-form documentary or explainer channel is a median RPM in the low single digits (about $2-6) for a global audience. $10-30+ RPM is achievable only with a heavily US/UK/Nordic/Australian audience in a high-intent vertical (finance, insurance, legal, SaaS, real estate), plus Q4 timing.
- Transport (cars and aviation) and Education & Science punch above their advertiser CPM because long videos carry more mid-rolls. This favors 15-30 minute documentary formats. For a faceless documentary team, "engineering / aviation / car-industry" and "science/economics explainers" are structurally good bets even outside finance.
- Tech and gadgets have a high CPM but a low RPM (only 34% of views monetized in AIR's data). This makes them less attractive than the headline CPM suggests unless the format is long-form analysis rather than quick reviews.
- Luxury (the friends' current jewel niche) has no reliable RPM dataset in any source found. The luxury audience is affluent, but luxury brands buy far less YouTube auction inventory than finance or insurance. Treat it as documentary/lifestyle-tier RPM unless proven otherwise.

### Gaps
- There is no verified 2025-26 dataset that splits "insurance", "law/legal", "real estate", "careers", "luxury", "geopolitics", "engineering" or "aviation" into separate median RPMs. The only figures for these are SEO ranges or AIR's pooled "Transport" and "News & Politics" categories.
- The original Business Insider creator-survey article and Reddit r/PartneredYoutube screenshot threads could not be retrieved (Reddit blocked this crawler; BI not fetched).
- The vidIQ and TubeBuddy niche-CPM pages returned 403 and were not read. The WordStream finance/insurance CPC figures were not captured.

## 2. How much lower is Spanish-language RPM, and the effect of Spain/US share

### Takeaway
Spanish-language content averages roughly 1/3 to 1/4 of English CPM, according to Lenos: Spanish CPM $3.00 and RPM $1.65, against English CPM $10.26 and RPM $5.64. The whole gap comes from audience geography. Spain pays roughly 3x Mexico and 4-5x Argentina, Colombia or Peru, while US viewers pay 3-5x Spain. A Spanish channel whose audience is 35-45% Spain plus 10% US can plausibly double its blended RPM compared with a typical Mexico/LatAm-heavy Spanish channel.

### Inferences
These are illustrative calculations of my own, using Lenos country RPMs for general content: Spain $3.91, US $10.81, Mexico $0.62, Argentina/Colombia about $0.56, other LatAm assumed $0.50. The US-Hispanic case uses an assumed $5.

| Audience mix | Blended general-content RPM |
|---|---|
| Typical LatAm-heavy Spanish channel (Spain 10%, US 5%, Mexico 30%, rest of LatAm 55%) | about $1.1-1.3 |
| Spain-heavy channel (Spain 35%, US 10%, Mexico 20%, rest of LatAm 35%) | about $2.2 (US at $5) to $2.8 (US at $10.81) |

- The Spain-heavy mix is roughly 2x the LatAm-heavy one. Every 10 points of audience moved from LatAm to Spain adds about +$0.33 RPM. Every 10 points moved to US viewers adds about +$0.45-1.0.
- Applying niche multipliers (finance 2.5-4x the general rate per FluxNote's LatAm multipliers) to a Spain-heavy finance/business channel suggests about $5-10 RPM. That is consistent with Spanish SEO sources quoting €8-14 for finance in Spain. Treat it as a directional estimate only.
- Spain's absolute RPM remains roughly 1/3 of the US, so even a Spain-heavy Spanish channel earns about 30-40% of an equivalent US-English channel per view. The Spanish market's advantage is lower competition, not rate.
- Castilian-Spain cues favor a Spain-heavy audience: vocabulary, references, € prices, Spanish companies, a peninsular voice. Neutral Latin American Spanish grows faster but dilutes RPM. This trade-off is central to the channel decision.

### Gaps
- No primary data (creator screenshots) splits US viewers of Spanish-language content from US viewers of English content. YouTube Analytics reports geography, not the viewer's language. The actual RPM of US-Hispanic viewers on Spanish-language long-form is unverified.
- No transparent Spain-specific RPM dataset by niche exists. All Spain niche figures come from Spanish SEO blogs without methodology.

## 3. Niches that keep a high RPM in Spanish, and reported Spanish-creator figures

### Takeaway
The same high-intent verticals (finance/investing, real estate, business/SaaS/tech, education) keep a premium in Spanish. They are the verticals with local Spanish advertisers: neobanks, brokers, fintech, insurers, SaaS, and Spain's banking/brokerage competition. Reported Spanish-language RPMs are roughly 1/3 to 1/2 of the English figures for the same niche. History and documentaries in Spanish sit around $2-6 RPM when the audience is Spain-heavy and under $1.5 when it is LatAm-heavy.

### Inferences
Ranking for a Spanish, faceless, long-form channel on AdSense RPM plus other levers:
1. Personal finance, investing, and "money stories" or business case studies with a Spain angle. This combines a high RPM with large affiliate potential from brokers and neobanks, but carries the highest policy risk if an AI persona gives advice (see section 4).
2. Business, economics and company case-study documentaries. These use a documentary format close to what the team already makes, draw finance/B2B advertisers, carry lower "advice" risk, and have strong long-video engagement.
3. Tech/AI/SaaS explainers with software affiliates. Their RPM is lower than their CPM suggests; beware the "unoriginal tutorial" policy clause.
4. Real estate and housing in Spain. RPM is high, but the audience is local, and the topic becomes finance/legal advice if framed as guidance.
5. Engineering, aviation and cars (documentary style). This group benefits from the mid-roll density found in AIR's Transport data and has low policy risk.
6. History, true crime and mystery. RPM is lower, but these are the most proven faceless Spanish formats, and also the most saturated with AI slop.

The existing jewels channel likely sits near the history/lifestyle band. A move toward "business of luxury" (brands, companies, auctions, investment value of jewels, watches or art) could shift the ad mix toward finance advertisers. This is a hypothesis to test with data from their current channel.

## 4. 2025-2026 YouTube policy environment for AI-assisted faceless channels

### Takeaway
AI voice and AI imagery are allowed and monetizable. What gets demonetized is content that is mass-produced, templated or low-variation, content without meaningful narration or commentary, "readings" of material the creator didn't write, emotionally manipulative formulas, and, since 16 July 2026, AI personas presenting themselves as human experts on health, legal, financial or political topics. Enforcement escalated from a rename in July 2025 to a termination wave in January 2026 (16 channels, 35M subscribers, 4.7B views) and a clarified three-category policy in July 2026. Anonymous faceless creators report collateral demonetizations. Separately, the Partner Program entry bar doubles to 8,000 watch hours for new applicants from 1 February 2027.

### Cited Findings

**Official policy text (YouTube Help, channel monetization policies)**
- **15 July 2025:** "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content" to clarify that it includes "content that is repetitive or mass-produced." YouTube says this content "has always been ineligible for monetization." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **Not allowed under inauthentic content:**
  - "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation across videos"
  - "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"
  - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective"
  - Characters in identical situations with the same outcomes
  - Source: [YouTube Help (EN)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [YouTube Help (EN-GB)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-GB)
- **Allowed:** "the same intro/outro with substantially different core content," or series where each video's substance is "materially varied and deliver[s] creative, educational or other value." — [YouTube Help (EN-GB)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-GB)
- **Current help page also includes the July 2026 subsections:**
  - "Unsatisfying or off-putting content": channels using "emotionally manipulative formulas," content made "to shock or surprise viewers for the sole purpose of getting views," or "deceptive or misleading imagery."
  - "AI personas on sensitive topics": "Channels uploading this content will not be allowed to monetise" if they present AI as a human expert giving health, legal, financial or political advice.
  - Source: [YouTube Help (EN-GB)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-GB)
- **Reused content:**
  - Not allowed: "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications," "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create," and "clips ... edited together with little or no narrative."
  - Allowed: critical reviews using clips, reactions with commentary, and "edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary."
  - Source: [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

**YouTube statements on the July 2025 change**
- Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie called it a "minor update" and said: "YouTube welcomes creators using AI tools to enhance storytelling, and channels that use AI in their content remain eligible for monetization." On reused content: "can continue to be monetized if you've added significant original commentary, modifications, or educational or entertainment value." Named targets included "narrative stories with only superficial differences" and "slideshows with identical narration." — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)

**July 2026 clarification (effective 16 July 2026)**
- VP of Trust & Safety Matt Halprin laid out three categories:
  1. Generic, repetitive or template-based content: "low-variation videos easily created with AI, CGI, or templates," **including "tutorial videos reproducing already-prevalent platform content."**
  2. Off-putting or distressing content, for example animals in distress followed by a rescue.
  3. AI personas discussing finance, legal, healthcare or medical topics.
- Enforcement: channels with excessive content in any category face YPP demonetization. Channels dedicated to off-putting content are removed from YPP entirely.
- Halprin: "AI can actually allow people to make a lot of videos... Sometimes those videos are great," but the same technology "enables stuff that's kind of content farming, and that's the stuff we don't want."
- Source: [TechCrunch, 20 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- YouTube's examples of AI personas include "An AI 'doctor' providing medical diagnoses...", "AI-generated podcast hosts offering financial guidance, investment tips, or wealth management advice," and "AI personas giving legal advice or interpreting laws." — [TechRepublic/search summary](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/off-putting-youtube-tighten-rules-104500810.html)

**Altered or synthetic content disclosure**
- Official rules (announced 18 Mar 2024): creators must disclose "realistic content – content a viewer could easily mistake for a real person, place, scene, or event" made with altered or synthetic media.
  - Requires disclosure: using a realistic person's likeness, including a synthetic voice; altering footage of real events or places; generating realistic scenes of fictional major events.
  - Does not require disclosure: "clearly unrealistic content, such as animation," colour or lighting filters, beauty filters, and generative AI "used for productivity, like generating scripts, content ideas, or automatic captions."
  - YouTube may add labels itself and planned enforcement against creators who "consistently choose not to disclose."
  - Source: [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)
- Secondary sources say disclosure does not reduce monetization or reach, that cloning your own voice needs no disclosure while cloning someone else's does, and that repeated non-disclosure can lead to YPP suspension. These are aggregator claims, consistent with YouTube's stance but not verified on an official page in this research. — [search summary of Narration Box / Minimatters / others](https://minimatters.com/youtube-altered-or-synthetic-content-disclosure/)

**Enforcement waves and reported demonetizations**

- Announced August 2026, effective **1 Feb 2027 for new applicants**: 1,000 subscribers plus **8,000 watch hours** in 12 months, or 20M Shorts views in 90 days, double the current bar. ### Inferences
- **Allowed, and the lowest-risk style for this team:** original, researched scripts with a clear thesis and narrative, distinct per video; human-written or human-edited narration; AI voice as a production tool; AI images used sparingly and disclosed when photorealistic; licensed stock/archive footage transformed by editing and commentary; varied structure and thumbnails.
  - A cloned voice of one of the founders is a stronger authenticity signal than a stock TTS voice. It also needs no disclosure, according to secondary sources.
- **Highest-risk styles in 2025-2026:**
  - Templated story channels: Bible stories, "reddit stories", fake-scenario drama, AI animated fan-fiction.
  - Slideshow or "top 10" compilations with stock footage and TTS.
  - Channels reading Wikipedia or articles verbatim.
  - High-frequency uploads (daily 20-minute documentaries) with uniform metadata.
  - AI-generated "expert" hosts in finance, health or legal. This clause matters most for the highest-RPM niches: a faceless finance channel must be framed as journalism, analysis or storytelling ("the story of how X went bankrupt"), not as a synthetic advisor ("I, your AI financial advisor, recommend...").
  - Unoriginal tutorials for AI tools that duplicate existing ones.
- **Spanish-language AI slop is a named enforcement target:** two of the headline January 2026 terminations were Spanish-titled channels. Spanish faceless formats (history, religion, mystery, cuentos) are therefore under scrutiny.
- **Timing:** a channel launched now that reaches 4,000 watch hours before 1 Feb 2027 enters YPP under the old bar. After that date it needs 8,000 hours. With 8-30 minute videos, 4,000 hours is about 24,000 views at 10 minutes average view duration. That is feasible in about 4 months only if the channel gets early traction, so it is an argument to launch quickly.

### Gaps
- YouTube has not published how many channels were demonetized or rejected under the inauthentic-content policy overall, nor any false-positive rates.
- The TechTimes list of "detection signals" and the claim that faceless channels are flagged are not confirmed by YouTube.
- The Hollywood Reporter article was paywalled, and details came via search snippets only.
- No data was found on YPP rejection rates specifically for Spanish-language faceless channels.

## 5. Other revenue levers per niche (affiliates, sponsorships), especially in Spanish

### Takeaway
In high-intent niches, AdSense is often the minority of revenue. Reported Spanish faceless finance and tech/SaaS cases earn 55-65% from affiliates, and sponsorships are priced at a $15-50 CPM, typically several times the channel's AdSense RPM. History and entertainment channels lack comparable affiliate products and depend 70-80% on AdSense. For a Spanish channel, this makes finance, business and SaaS far more lucrative than their AdSense RPM alone suggests.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube sponsorship rates:** many deals run at a $15-50 CPM, with high-value niches above that. The Spanish creator average is cited at €4.50 CPM, up to €15 in finance, insurance, tech and training, although it is unclear whether this is an ad or sponsor CPM. Micro-channels with 10K-50K subscribers charge €200-2,000 per sponsored video in Spain. Combining AdSense, affiliates and sponsorships can multiply income 2-3x. LOW-MEDIUM confidence: aggregator/SEO sources. — [CreatorsJet (ES)](https://www.creatorsjet.com/es/blog/cuanto-pagan-las-marcas-por-patrocinios-de-youtube); [AceleraTusRedes](https://aceleratusredes.com/blog/cuanto-paga-youtube-en-espana)
- **Revenue mixes from anonymous Spanish cases:**
  - Spain finance channel: 65% affiliates, 35% AdSense
  - Spain tech/SaaS: 55% SaaS affiliates, 30% sponsorships, 15% AdSense
  - Mexico AI-voice history-documentary channel: 80% AdSense, 20% memberships
  - Source: [YouViral](https://youviral.app/en/blog/canal-faceless-youtube-cuanto-gana)
- **Spanish finance affiliate ecosystem:** the brokers and neobanks active in Spain include Trade Republic (€1 per trade, with a Spanish-IBAN banking branch authorized in June 2025), MyInvestor (Spanish bank supervised by the Bank of Spain and CNMV) and eToro ($50 minimum deposit). These are the natural sponsors and affiliate partners for a Spanish finance channel, but per-client affiliate payouts were not found. — [HelpMyCash](https://www.helpmycash.com/invertir/mejor-broker-para-empezar-invertir-principiantes/); [Rankia](https://www.rankia.com/blog/mejores-brokers/6736089-trade-republic-vs-myinvestyor)
- AIR notes Business & Finance has a +60% Q4 RPM swing, and that finance advertisers "spend hard into year-end." Sponsor budgets likely follow the same seasonality, which is an inference. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)
- The 2027 YPP changes route more Premium Lite revenue to creators (60% of net), which AIR reports. This modestly raises non-ad income for long-form in all niches. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1-million-views)

### Inferences
Rough per-niche lever map, based on the sources above plus general market structure (not verified numerically):

| Niche | Main non-AdSense levers |
|---|---|
| Finance/investing (Spain) | Broker, neobank and fintech affiliates and sponsors; the highest total revenue per view |
| Business case studies | Sponsorships from B2B, SaaS and education platforms; newsletter; courses |
| Tech/AI/SaaS | Recurring SaaS affiliate commissions; tool sponsors |
| Real estate | Local lead generation; mortgage and insurance affiliates (Spain only) |
| History/true crime/documentary | Memberships, Patreon, audiobook or VPN-type sponsors; mostly AdSense |
| Luxury/jewels | Low sponsor density in Spanish; possible watch or jewelry e-commerce affiliates |

- Because affiliate conversion depends on the country, a Spain-focused finance channel can monetize Spanish products that don't serve LatAm viewers. A pan-Hispanic audience needs products available across borders, such as eToro, Revolut or international SaaS.

### Gaps
- No verified per-client affiliate payouts (CPA) were found for Spanish brokers or neobanks (Trade Republic, MyInvestor, eToro, Revolut, Indexa), nor Amazon.es commission rates by category.
- No verified Spanish-language sponsorship rate cards by niche were found; the figures above are from SEO or aggregator pages.
- Spain's regulatory limits on finance or crypto promotion by creators were not researched here (for example, CNMV crypto-advertising rules and Spain's 2024 influencer decree). They could restrict affiliate monetization in the finance niche and should be checked separately.
